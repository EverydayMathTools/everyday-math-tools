Percentage Calculators: Complete Guide

Percentages are used in everyday life, including shopping, education, finance and business. Our percentage calculators help you solve common percentage problems quickly and easily.

1. What Is a Percentage?

A percentage represents a number as a part of 100. It is commonly used to compare values, calculate marks, find discounts and understand changes in quantities.

Formula

Percentage = (Part / Total) × 100

Example

If you score 80 marks out of 100, your percentage is 80%.

2. How to Calculate a Percentage of a Number

To find a percentage of a number, divide the percentage by 100 and multiply it by the given number.

Formula

Result = (Percentage / 100) × Number

Example

20% of 150 = 30.

3. Percentage Increase

Percentage increase shows how much a value has grown compared with its original value.

Formula

Percentage Increase = ((New Value - Old Value) / Old Value) × 100

Example

If a price increases from 100 to 150, the increase is 50%.

4. Percentage Decrease

Percentage decrease measures how much a value has fallen compared with its original value.

Formula

Percentage Decrease = ((Old Value - New Value) / Old Value) × 100

Example

If a price decreases from 200 to 150, the decrease is 25%.

5. Percentage Change

Percentage change tells you whether a value has increased or decreased compared with its previous value.

Formula

Percentage Change = ((New Value - Old Value) / Absolute Old Value) × 100

Example

If a value changes from 100 to 130, it has increased by 30%.

6. Discount Calculation

A discount is a reduction in the original price of a product. You can calculate how much money you save and the final price.

Formula

Discount = (Original Price × Discount Rate) / 100

Final Price = Original Price - Discount

Example

A 20% discount on a price of 2,000 saves 400. The final price is 1,600.

7. Marks Percentage

Students use marks percentage to find their academic performance based on obtained marks and total marks.

Formula

Marks Percentage = (Obtained Marks / Total Marks) × 100

Example

If you obtain 450 marks out of 500, your percentage is 90%.

8. What Percentage Is One Number of Another?

This calculation helps you find what percentage one number represents of another number.

Formula

Percentage = (First Number / Second Number) × 100

Example

30 is 20% of 150.

9. Percentage Difference

Percentage difference compares two values using their average, without treating either value as the original.

Formula

Percentage Difference = (Absolute Difference / Average of Absolute Values) × 100

Example

The percentage difference between 100 and 120 is 18.18%.

10. Reverse Percentage

Reverse percentage helps you find an original value when you know the final value after a percentage increase or decrease.

Formula

After Increase: Original Value = Final Value / (1 + Rate / 100)

After Decrease: Original Value = Final Value / (1 - Rate / 100)

Example

If a price after a 20% discount is 800, its original price was 1,000.

Academic Calculators: Complete Guide

Understanding your academic performance helps you set goals and track your progress. These guides explain how GPA, CGPA and grades are calculated.

1. What Is GPA (Grade Point Average)?

GPA is a numerical representation of a student's academic performance during a semester. It is calculated using grade points and the credit hours of each subject.

GPA Formula

GPA = Total (Grade Points × Credit Hours) / Total Credit Hours

Example

Suppose you study two subjects with equal credit hours. Your grade points are 4.0 and 3.0. Your GPA is 3.50.

Important Note

Grade point scales can vary between universities. Our calculator currently uses a general 4.0 grading scale.

2. What Is CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average)?

CGPA represents your overall academic performance across multiple semesters. It considers the GPA and credit hours of each semester.

CGPA Formula

CGPA = Total (Semester GPA × Semester Credit Hours) / Total Credit Hours

Example

If your first semester GPA is 3.50 with 15 credit hours and your second semester GPA is 4.00 with 15 credit hours, your CGPA is 3.75.

Important Note

For an accurate CGPA, enter the credit hours completed in each semester. University grading policies may differ.

3. How to Calculate Your Grade?

A grade represents your academic performance based on the percentage of marks you achieve. Grading systems differ between schools, colleges and universities.

Percentage Formula

Percentage = (Obtained Marks / Total Marks) × 100

Example

If you obtain 85 marks out of 100, your percentage is 85%.

Important Note

Our Grade Calculator currently uses a general grading scale. Actual grade boundaries may vary by institution.

Exam and Grade Planning: Complete Guide

Planning your academic performance can help you understand what scores you need to achieve your desired results. These calculators make grade calculations easier.

1. Final Grade Calculator

A Final Grade Calculator helps you find the score required in your final exam to achieve a target overall grade. It uses your current coursework grade and the final exam weight.

Formula

Required Final Exam Score = [Target Grade - (Current Grade × (1 - Final Exam Weight / 100))] / (Final Exam Weight / 100)

Example

If your current grade is 75%, the final exam is worth 40%, and your target overall grade is 80%, you need 87.50% in the final exam.

Important Note

Enter your average percentage for completed coursework as your current grade. The final exam weight should represent its share of the total course grade.

2. Weighted Grade Calculator

A Weighted Grade Calculator calculates your overall grade when assignments, quizzes, projects and exams have different percentage weights.

Formula

Weighted Grade = Sum of (Score × Weight) / Total Weight

Example

If your assignment score is 80% with a 20% weight, your quiz score is 90% with a 30% weight, and your exam score is 70% with a 50% weight, your weighted grade is 78%.

Important Note

For a complete course grade, the weights should add up to 100%. Make sure you enter each score and its corresponding weight correctly.

3. Exam Marks Calculator

An Exam Marks Calculator helps you find how many additional marks you need to reach a target percentage based on your total marks and marks already earned.

Formula

Required Marks = (Target Percentage / 100 × Total Marks) - Marks Already Earned

Example

If the total marks are 100, you have already earned 35 marks, and your target is 80%, you need 45 more marks.

Important Note

Enter the total marks for the complete assessment and the marks you have already earned toward that total. Your target must be achievable with the remaining marks.
